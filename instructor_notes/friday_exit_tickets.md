# Friday exit tickets and interpretation guide

## Session 1: theory

### Prompt

A fitted annual relationship has slope 0.70 local °C per global-anomaly °C and positive
correlation. Write one claim this supports and one claim it does not support. The two series both
rise through time: name one plot or sensitivity check you need before trusting the summary.

### Strong-response ingredients

- supports a sample-bounded association: across the selected matched years, a 1 °C higher NASA
  global anomaly is associated with approximately 0.70 °C higher fitted annual Pier SST;
- does not establish that the global index directly caused the local change, that the slope isolates
  CO₂ or another forcing, or that it transfers to another site/period;
- asks for both series in time, a year-colored scatterplot, residuals in time, levels versus
  year-to-year changes, completeness/threshold sensitivity, or another relevant check; and
- recognizes that first differences change the question and do not automatically solve confounding
  or dependence.

## Session 2: submission reflection

### Prompt

In two sentences: name one consequential choice you made in your analysis and one limitation that
would matter before using the result in a research claim.

### Strong-response ingredients

- a concrete choice such as source version, completeness threshold, period, variable, flag rule,
  matching rule, statistic, fitted direction, or sensitivity case; and
- a limitation connected to source revision, sampling, dependence, missingness, shared trend,
  confounding, generalizability, or execution failure.

## Common misconceptions for later follow-up

- “High correlation means strong causation.” Association is not design, mechanism, or attribution.
- “NASA's anomaly is an absolute global temperature.” It is a deviation from a stated baseline.
- “A slope is unitless.” Its units are response units per predictor unit.
- “Residual means bad observation.” It is observed minus fitted; large values require investigation.
- “No residual curve means the model is true.” Diagnostics can reveal problems, not prove all
  assumptions.
- “Annual values are automatically independent.” Climate persistence and trend can remain annually.
- “Differencing removes every trend and confounder.” It changes the question and has its own
  assumptions.
- “The Pier–CO₂ slope is the effect of CO₂.” A local contemporaneous bivariate fit is not a climate
  attribution model.
