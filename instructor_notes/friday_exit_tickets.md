# Friday exit tickets and interpretation guide

## Session 1: theory

### Prompt

A fitted MOP relationship has slope 0.10 m/s and positive correlation. Write one claim this supports
and one claim it does not support. Name one plot/check you need before trusting the summary.

### Strong-response ingredients

- supports a sample-bounded association: within the selected file, a one-second higher peak period
  is associated with approximately 0.10 m higher fitted significant wave height;
- does not establish that period causes height, that the relation holds elsewhere/long-term, or that
  errors are independent; and
- asks for the scatterplot, residual plot, time plot, grouping/sensitivity view, metadata/flags, or
  another relevant check.

## Session 2: submission reflection

### Prompt

In two sentences: name one consequential choice you made in your analysis and one limitation that
would matter before using the result in a research claim.

### Strong-response ingredients

- a concrete choice such as period, variable, flag rule, grouping, comparison, statistic, interval,
  fitted direction, or sensitivity case; and
- a limitation connected to source, sampling, dependence, missingness, model output, confounding,
  circular direction, generalizability, or execution failure.

## Common misconceptions for later follow-up

- “High correlation means strong causation.” Association is not design/mechanism.
- “A slope is unitless.” Its units are response units per predictor unit.
- “Residual means bad observation.” It is observed minus fitted; large values require investigation.
- “No residual curve means the model is true.” Diagnostics can reveal problems, not prove all
  assumptions.
- “Thousands of hourly values are thousands of independent events.” Persistence/autocorrelation
  matters.
- “Direction averages like any other number.” Angles wrap at 360° and require metadata/circular
  treatment.
