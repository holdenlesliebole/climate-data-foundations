# Thursday exit tickets and interpretation guide

Use these as low-stakes evidence for Friday pairing and review.

## Session 1: theory

### Prompt

A temperature-difference sample has standard deviation 1.3 °C and estimated standard error of the
mean 0.2 °C. Explain what each number describes. Name one assumption behind the standard error.

### Strong-response ingredients

- 1.3 °C describes spread among individual observed differences;
- 0.2 °C describes estimated repeated-sample variation of the mean under the stated model; and
- independence/exchangeability, common process/distribution, representative sampling, or another
  correctly bounded assumption is named.

### Follow-up cue

If a student says only “one is more accurate,” ask: “Which collection of values would each number be
the spread of?”

## Session 2: application

### Prompt

Complete: “My estimate is ___; I resampled ___; this treats ___ as exchangeable; and the interval
does not address ___.”

### Strong-response ingredients

- identifies the mean summer surface-minus-bottom difference (or their explicitly changed target);
- identifies years/summers as the resampling unit;
- states that the selected years are treated as exchangeable/representative under the bootstrap
  model; and
- names bias, missingness, sensor/method change, within-year mechanism, limited years, or another
  limitation not repaired by the interval.

## Common misconceptions to address Friday

- “The standard deviation is uncertainty in the mean.” It is observation spread; standard error or
  a bootstrap distribution targets estimate variability.
- “Bootstrap creates new evidence.” It reuses observed units to approximate variation under a
  model.
- “More rows means more independent samples.” Time dependence can make effective information much
  smaller.
- “A 95% interval contains 95% of observations.” That confuses estimate uncertainty with the data
  distribution.
- “The narrowest interval is the best method.” Width is partly a consequence of assumptions.

## Confidence pulse

Green/yellow/red for: (1) distinguishing SD and SE, (2) naming a resampling unit, (3) interpreting a
bootstrap interval, and (4) explaining why adjacent days may be dependent.
